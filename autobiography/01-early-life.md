Chapter 1: My Early Life and Background

My name is Champ Muhlenberg. I was born in Liberia and grew up in Police Academy, Paynesville, Montserrado County.

My childhood was not easy. My family experienced financial difficulties, and at one point, we found ourselves in an orphanage while trying to continue our education. My mother was sick, and my father became affected by the war. After his experiences in the bush, he developed a medical condition, which made our family circumstances even more difficult.

My brother and I experienced interruptions in our education. I attended a government school, but without enough academic support, my performance began to decline.

Later, I transferred to Life Foundation. This became an important turning point in my educational journey.

My brother, Freddy Andrew, regularly called me, encouraged me, and helped me with my studies. My sister also supported my education and helped pay my school fees. Their sacrifices and encouragement made a significant difference in my life.

Through determination and their support, I improved academically and continued my education until I graduated from Bishop Matthew Norwood High School.

After graduation, I received an opportunity to travel from Liberia to Rwanda to continue my education.

Looking back, I understand that my beginnings were difficult, but those experiences taught me perseverance, gratitude, and the importance of education.

My journey did not end with the challenges of childhood. It was only the beginning of a bigger dream.
